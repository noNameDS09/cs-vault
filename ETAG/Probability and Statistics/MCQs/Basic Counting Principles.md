## 📋 Level 1: Basic Application (Addition & Multiplication Rules)

## 📋 Level 2: Advanced — Restrictions, Conditions & Mixed Principles

**1.** A student has 4 different shirts, 3 different trousers, and 2 different pairs of shoes. However, one particular shirt cannot be worn with one particular pair of trousers. How many valid outfits are possible?  
A) 24  
B) 22  
C) 23  
D) 20  

**2.** A code consists of 3 positions. The first position can be one of 4 letters, the second one of 3 digits, and the third one can be either of 2 symbols or 3 letters. How many different codes are possible?  
A) 60  
B) 72  
C) 120  
D) 96  

**3.** A student wants to choose one course from Mathematics (3 options), one from Computer Science (4 options), and one from Electronics (2 options). However, if a particular Mathematics course is chosen, only 1 of the Electronics courses is allowed. How many valid combinations are possible?  
A) 20  
B) 22  
C) 23  
D) 24  

**4.** A travel plan from A to D must pass through either B or C, but not both. There are 3 ways from A to B and 4 ways from B to D. There are 2 ways from A to C and 5 ways from C to D. How many travel plans are possible?  
A) 20  
B) 22  
C) 26  
D) 32  

**5.** A password consists of a letter, followed by a digit, followed by a letter. There are 5 choices for each letter and 4 choices for the digit. If the two letters must be different, how many passwords are possible?  
A) 80  
B) 100  
C) 90  
D) 60  

**6.** A menu allows a customer to choose either:  
- one item from 5 starters and one item from 4 main courses, **or**  
- one item from 3 sandwiches and one drink from 2 options.  

How many possible selections are there?  
A) 26  
B) 28  
C) 30  
D) 32  

**7.** A student has to create a 3-part project. For Part 1 there are 3 choices, for Part 2 there are 4 choices, and for Part 3 there are 5 choices. However, if the first choice in Part 1 is selected, only 2 choices are available for Part 2. How many projects are possible?  
A) 50  
B) 52  
C) 56  
D) 60  

**8.** A company has 3 offices in City A, 4 offices in City B, and 2 offices in City C. An employee must choose an office in City A and then an office in either City B or City C. How many possible pairs of offices can be chosen?  
A) 18  
B) 20  
C) 24  
D) 30  

**9.** A 4-character code is formed using the choices below:  
- First character: 3 possible letters  
- Second character: 4 possible digits  
- Third character: either 2 possible letters or 3 possible digits  
- Fourth character: 5 possible symbols  

How many codes are possible?  
A) 100  
B) 120  
C) 150  
D) 180  

**10.** A student can reach a destination by either Route A or Route B. Route A has 3 first-stage choices and 4 second-stage choices. Route B has 2 first-stage choices and 5 second-stage choices. However, one combination in Route A is unavailable. How many possible journeys are there?  
A) 23  
B) 24  
C) 25  
D) 26  

**11.** There are 4 types of laptops, 3 types of operating systems, and 2 types of storage options. For one particular laptop, only one operating system is compatible. For all other laptops, all operating systems are compatible. How many valid configurations are possible?  
A) 20  
B) 21  
C) 22  
D) 24  

**12.** A task can be performed in one of three ways:  
- Method A: 3 choices followed by 4 choices  
- Method B: 5 choices  
- Method C: 2 choices followed by 3 choices  

If the three methods are mutually exclusive, how many total possibilities are there?  
A) 24  
B) 25  
C) 27  
D) 29

## 📐 Key Formulae Summary (For Quick Revision)

| Principle | Formula | When to Use |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| **Addition Rule** | $n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B)$ | Choices are **mutually exclusive** (OR) — pick **one** from disjoint sets |
| **Multiplication Rule** | $n(A \times B) = n(A) \times n(B)$ | Choices are **independent** (AND) — pick **one from each** set sequentially |
| **Generalized Multiplication** | $n(A_1 \times A_2 \times \dots \times A_k) = \prod_{i=1}^k n(A_i)$ | $k$ independent stages, one choice per stage |
| **Restriction/Exclusion** | $N_{\text{valid}} = N_{\text{total}} - N_{\text{invalid}}$ | Subtract forbidden combinations from total |
| **Conditional Counting** | $N = \sum N_{\text{case }i}$ | Break into **mutually exclusive cases** (casework), compute each, then add |
| **Mixed (Add + Multiply)** | $N = \sum \prod n(\text{stage})$ | Each case uses multiplication; cases combined by addition |

## 🎯 Quick Decision Guide

| Question Phrasing | Likely Principle |
|-------------------|------------------|
| "either ... or ..." / "choose one from" | **Addition** |
| "and" / "followed by" / "one from each" | **Multiplication** |
| "cannot be together" / "except" / "invalid" | **Total − Invalid** |
| "if ... then only ..." / "depends on" | **Casework (Conditional)** |
| "either Route A or Route B, each has stages" | **Add (Multiply per route)** |
